THE ANCIENT BRAIN — "What Happens to the Human Brain During Total Darkness"
10 THUMBNAIL CONCEPTS + IMAGE PROMPTS
=======================================================================


WHAT MAKES THESE WORK (STUDIED FROM YOUR REFERENCE THUMBNAILS):
- Bold 2-4 word text that creates a curiosity gap
- High contrast (black/dark background + bright yellow or white pop)
- One dominant visual element the eye sees in under 1 second
- A character (stickman or face) that creates human connection
- Emotional tension — fear, confusion, shock, mystery

THUMBNAIL 1 — "THE GLITCH BRAIN"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Text overlay: YOUR BRAIN GLITCHES (top, bold yellow with thick black outline)

Why it works: "Glitch" is a high-CTR word. Relatable modern language applied to
a biological event = irresistible curiosity gap. Algorithm favours videos that
borrow trending language.

Image prompt:
Hand-drawn 2D stickman / MS Paint doodle style. Pure black background. Centre
of frame: a large cartoon brain shape (irregular oval, squiggly fold lines on
top surface) filled with flat yellow — but the RIGHT HALF of the brain is
visually glitching: jagged horizontal scan lines cutting through it, random
pixel-block squares scattered across the surface, two disconnected brain chunks
floating apart with a visible crack down the middle. The LEFT HALF is normal
yellow. The RIGHT HALF has navy blue static patches and flat red pixel squares.
Below the brain: a small stickman figure (medium circle head, tiny filled white
dot eyes — wide with shock, small open O-mouth of disbelief). The stickman's
arms are raised straight upward at 90 degrees on both sides — maximum alarm.
Legs spread wide apart. The stickman is looking directly up at the glitching
brain. Top of frame: "YOUR BRAIN GLITCHES" in enormous bold all-caps letters —
flat yellow fill, thick black outline, slightly uneven hand-drawn edges.
Bottom-left corner: small "in TOTAL DARKNESS" in smaller white caps. Flat
colour only, no gradients, no glow, MS Paint doodle aesthetic.

THUMBNAIL 2 — "THE TRAPPED STICKMAN EYE"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Text overlay: 3 DAYS. NO LIGHT. (left side, stacked, white bold)

Why it works: Directly mirrors the "DO THEY KNOW?" eye thumbnail style but
makes it darker, more personal, and more specific. The number "3 DAYS" creates
an immediate time-bound stakes hook.

Image prompt:
Hand-drawn 2D stickman / MS Paint doodle style. Full flat black background.
Dominating 80% of the frame: a single enormous eye shape — an almond/lens
shape drawn with thick black outline, filled with flat bright yellow for the
iris. The pupil is a massive black circle taking up the centre third of the
eye — representing extreme dilation in darkness. Inside the black pupil: a tiny
stickman figure, white outline only against the black, curled slightly inward —
knees bent, both arms wrapped around the body (hugging itself — isolated,
frightened). The stickman's circle head is tilted downward, dot eyes directed
downward and inward (dejected, lost). The eye has four short radiating lash
lines at the top and bottom (simple tick marks — hand-drawn style). Left side
of the frame, outside the eye: "3 DAYS." in massive bold white caps stacked
vertically. Below it: "NO LIGHT." in the same size, flat red fill with black
outline. Flat colour only, MS Paint doodle aesthetic.

THUMBNAIL 3 — "THE BEFORE / AFTER SPLIT"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Text overlay: HOUR 1 VS HOUR 72 (centred top, yellow)

Why it works: Before/after comparisons are among the highest-CTR thumbnail
formats on YouTube. Specific numbers (1 vs 72) feel credible and scientific —
perfect for a brain/science channel.

Image prompt:
Hand-drawn 2D stickman / MS Paint doodle style. Frame split down the exact
centre by a thick bold black vertical dividing line. LEFT HALF — white
background. Label at top in black caps: "HOUR 1." Single stickman centred in
the left half: medium circle head (clean circle outline, white fill), two calm
half-moon dot eyes (relaxed, curved upward slightly), gentle upward-curve mouth
(content). Body straight and tall. Arms hanging loosely at 20 degrees from
sides. Legs shoulder-width apart, relaxed stance. A small yellow sun shape in
the upper left corner (circle with 6 radiating lines). RIGHT HALF — full flat
navy blue / near-black background. Label at top in white caps: "HOUR 72." Same
stickman but deteriorated: medium circle head now slightly tilted 15 degrees
sideways, eyes ENORMOUS white circles with tiny black pupils (maximum fear
dilation), jagged zigzag mouth (distress and trembling), body line curved
(hunched), both arms raised upward and outward in alarm, legs spread wide and
bent at knees (unstable). Faint scribble marks around the head suggesting noise
or chaos (three loose spiral lines). Centre top spanning both halves: "HOUR 1
VS HOUR 72" in bold flat yellow all-caps with thick black outline. Flat colour
only, MS Paint doodle style.

THUMBNAIL 4 — "THE BROKEN CLOCK"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Text overlay: HE LOST 33 DAYS (large, top, red)

Why it works: A real number from a real experiment (Siffre's 1962 study) sounds
unbelievable — which is exactly why people click. Specific data in thumbnails
outperforms vague claims every time.

Image prompt:
Hand-drawn 2D stickman / MS Paint doodle style. Black background. Dominating
the right two-thirds of the frame: a large wall clock shape (circle outline,
thick black border, clock face with 12 tick marks for hours, clock hands
pointing to roughly 3:00). But the clock is BROKEN — a bold jagged crack runs
diagonally from upper-left to lower-right across the entire clock face,
splitting it into two sections that are pulling apart (a visible gap between the
two halves, each drifting slightly in opposite directions). The hour and minute
hands are bent and misaligned. The clock face has a flat white fill on the left
section and a flat navy fill on the right section — showing day and night
disconnecting. Left side of the frame: a stickman with medium circle head, dot
eyes wide (two large filled circles), downturned curved mouth (devastated), one
hand raised toward the clock (arm stick extended forward at 30 degrees, pointing
at the crack), other arm hanging at side. Legs together, body straight — frozen
in disbelief. Top of frame: "HE LOST 33 DAYS" in enormous bold caps — flat red
fill, thick black outline, slightly hand-lettered uneven edges. Below in smaller
white caps: "underground. no light." Flat colour, MS Paint doodle aesthetic.

THUMBNAIL 9 — "THE TWO TIMELINES"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Text overlay: HE THOUGHT IT WAS DAY 25 / IT WAS DAY 58

Why it works: The Siffre time compression story is the most share-worthy moment
in the video. Two contradicting facts in one thumbnail creates cognitive
dissonance — the brain must resolve it by clicking.

Image prompt:
Hand-drawn 2D stickman / MS Paint doodle style. White background. Two large
calendar grid shapes side by side (each calendar: a simple rectangle with a
grid of small squares — 5 columns x 5 rows of date boxes, a header bar at top).
LEFT CALENDAR: header bar filled flat yellow, labelled "HE THOUGHT:" in black
caps. Inside the grid, 25 boxes are marked with a simple X — the 25th box has
a bold circle around it. The calendar has a normal, clean appearance. RIGHT
CALENDAR: header bar filled flat red, labelled "REALITY:" in white caps. Inside
the grid, all 58 boxes are marked — the grid overflows (boxes spilling beyond
the rectangle border with more X marks, suggesting the true number is
overwhelming). The 58th box circled in red. Between the two calendars: a
stickman in the centre — medium circle head, dot eyes pointing left and right
simultaneously (one eye looking left at the yellow calendar, one eye looking
right at the red calendar — a cross-eyed confused expression), mouth a flat
horizontal dash of confusion. Both arms are outstretched toward each calendar
(arm sticks pointing left and right). Legs together, standing between them. Top
of frame: "HE THOUGHT IT WAS DAY 25" in large bold black caps. Below that: "IT
WAS DAY 58" in same size but flat red with black outline — underlined. Flat
colour, MS Paint doodle style.

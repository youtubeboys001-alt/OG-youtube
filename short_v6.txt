VIDEO 6 SHORT — "The Village Where Everyone Kept Falling Asleep"
Voice: Brian (ai33pro) | ~1.5 min | Hard hook, hard cut, no outro | Plain text

---

An entire village kept falling asleep — for days at a time — and for years, nobody could explain why.

It's a real place. Kalachi, in northern Kazakhstan. A quiet village on the empty steppe. Then, around 2013, people started dropping. Not tired. Not fainting. Switched off. A woman would slump over mid-milking. A child would lie down in the road walking home. And they wouldn't wake — not for hours, but for two days, three days, sometimes a full week.

And they came back wrong. Memory gaps. Crushing headaches. And the children woke describing the same hallucinations — flying horses, worms crawling from their hands — kids who'd never spoken to each other, seeing the same impossible things.

It hit one in seven people. Some of them again and again. And every theory failed. Mass hysteria? The brain scans showed real swelling — you can't fake that. Bad vodka? Toddlers were falling asleep too. A virus? They found nothing.

And then the obvious suspect. Right next to the village sits an abandoned Soviet uranium mine. A sleeping sickness next to a uranium mine — it had to be radiation. They tested everything. It came back clean.

The answer wasn't radiation. It was the air.

The abandoned mines had flooded. And underground, in the dark, they were quietly eating the oxygen and breathing out carbon monoxide — invisible, odorless — seeping up and pooling over the village. Carbon monoxide gives no warning. The brain just gets quieter, and quieter, and switches off.

The town was putting itself to sleep. So in 2015, they evacuated it.

Kalachi sits mostly empty now — a place its own air made uninhabitable.

The question is how many other places are still waiting.
